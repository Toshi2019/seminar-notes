
%\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Microlocal sheaf theory allows us to
study the propagation of cohomology 
of complexes of sheaves. 
A central notion in the theory 
is that of microsupport of a complex of sheaves. 
It was defined by Kashiwara and Schapira 
in 1982~\cite{KS82}. 

Let \(M\) be a real manifold 
and \(F\) a complex of sheaves. 
Then the microsupport \(\muS[](F)\) of \(F\) is 
a closed conic (i.e., stable 
under \(\rr^{+}\)-action) coisotropic subset 
of the cotangent bundle of \(M\). 
Roughly speaking, \(\muS[](F)\) describes the (co)directions 
of \(M\) in which the cohomology of some degree of \(F\) cannot be extended. 
Note that we cannnot regain from \(\muS[](F)\) information of
the cohomological degree of \(F\) where 
its cohomology does not propagate.

%\subsection{Truncated Microsupport}}
However, 
there are certain propagation phenomena which depend on 
the degree of cohomology. 
One cannot treat such phenomena by microsupports. 
To address this difficulty, 
Kashiwara, Monteiro-Fernandes, and Schapira~\cite{KMS03a} 
introduced the notion of truncated microsupport. 
This notion encodes information about singularities 
in cotangent directions depending on the degree of cohomology. 

Both notions above encode microlocal information. 
Then how can we deduce 
the global propagation results from this microlocal information? 
For ordinary microsupport,
an answer is given by the non-characteristic 
deformation lemma due to Kashiwara~\cite{Kas75}. 
This lemma plays a fundamental role 
in microlocal sheaf theory, and 
is used throughout \cite{KS90}. 

In this paper, we prove a truncated version 
of the non-characteristic deformation lemma
with slight modifications. 
More precisely, 
we make two modifications 
to the lemma. 

First, we weaken the hypothesis of vanishing of 
cohomologies. In the original lemma, we assume that 
the cohomologies of all degrees vanish. 
In our version, 
it is required only in degrees at most a fixed integer.

Second, we make an additional assumption on 
the deformation family.
Note that 
we cannot apply the original proof of the lemma
to our truncated version. 
Indeed we give an example 
showing that 
the conclusion of the truncated version can fail
without this additional assumption.

To make the last
assumption acceptable to the reader, 
we give several examples of deformation families
with figures. 


As an application, 
we prove a truncated version of 
the microlocal Morse lemma for a morphism of manifolds.
By considering the sheaf of hyperfunction 
solutions of a \(\mathcal{D}\)-module, 
one can obtain a global version of 
Holmgren's uniqueness theorem. 

\paragraph{Analytic point of view} 

From the viewpoint of propagation, 
our results are along the lines of 
the Cauchy-Kowalevsky theorem. 
In 1971, Zerner showed a theorem on analytic extension of 
solutions of a single differential equation 
whose coefficients are holomorphic \cite{Zer71}. 
In 1972, Bony and Schapira generalized the result to 
the case of families of differential operators \cite{BS72}. 
In 1975, Kashiwara used this method and 
the Mittag-Leffler argument for sheaves to extend the result 
to the case of the solution complex of 
a coherent \(\mathcal{D}\)-module~\cite{Kas75}. 
Note that Kashiwara used this method to prove 
the constructibility theorem:  
if \(\mathcal{M}\) is a holonomic \(\mathcal{D}\)-module,
the solution complex \(
    \RHOM_{\mathcal{D}_{X}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{X})
\) is constructible.

Our result in the case of hyperfunction solutions can be seen 
as a truncated version of these propagation results.







\paragraph{Organization}

The paper is organized as follows. 
In Section \ref{sec:review}, 
we review notation and results from \cite{KS90}. 
In Section \ref{sec:t-ss}, 
we recall the definition of truncated microsupport, 
introduced in \cite{KMS03a}. 
In Section \ref{sec:t-nonchar}, 
we prove a truncated version of 
the noncharacteristic deformation lemma. 
In the proof, Lemma \ref{clm:ind-nonchar} plays a crucial role. 
In Section \ref{sec:t-morse}, 
we apply the result in Section \ref{sec:t-nonchar} 
to microlocal Morse theory. 
Finally in Appendix \ref{sec:special-isom}, 
we give some technical results.

In this paper, 
in contrast to the previous research, 
we shall apply the truncated microsupport 
to the study of some geometrical problems. 
First we prove a truncated version of 
the noncharacteristic deformation lemma. 
Consider a Hausdorff space and a family of open subsets of it. 
Assume that the family is ``noncharacteristic'' with 
respect to a complex of sheaves on the space.
In this case, 
the original version of the theorem asserts that 
the global section on a member of the family 
is isomorphic to the one on the union of the family. 
Note that this isomorphism is one between derived functors.

%
This lemma is one of the most fundamental 
results in microlocal sheaf theory, and 
used throughout \cite{KS90}.
Thus a truncated version of this lemma 
is also the key result in this paper. 
However, one should notice that 
the original proof of the lemma cannot be applied 
to our truncated version. 
To prove the result, 
we make three modifications 
to the lemma. 
First, we add an assumption on a total space. 
That is, 
we assume that the space is not only Hausdorff, but also 
satisfies the condition that 
all the open subspaces are paracompact.
Second, we weaken the hypothesis of vanishing of 
cohomologies. In the original lemma, we assume that 
the cohomologies of all degrees vanish. However, in our version, 
we assume that the cohomologies of not all degrees 
but degrees less than a certain number vanish.
Third, we make an additional assumption on 
the family of deformation.
Therefore, 
to make, in particular, the third
assumption acceptable to the reader, 
we give several examples of families of deformation with figures. 
Then, in turn, we formulate 
a truncated version (Theorem \ref{thm:t-morse}) of

under the light of truncated microsupport.
